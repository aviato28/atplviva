@ 1
CH: ACARS - the full story
S: Tell me about ACARS.
A: Aircraft Communications Addressing and Reporting System - digital data link for short messages between aircraft and ground over VHF, HF or SATCOM; also used for flight tracking.
S: What does it actually do for the operator?
A: Auto-reports flight phase changes, interfaces with the FMS (flight plan/weather uplink), and sends real-time system and maintenance fault data.
S: Say ACARS is unserviceable before departure - what now?
A: Operator must ensure real-time tracking using ADS-B. ACARS/ADS-B must be functional before every departure, and crew must never switch it off in flight.
S: What if you fly where neither has coverage?
A: Operator devises a tracking procedure; crew report coordinates, speed and altitude at intervals not exceeding 15 minutes.
@ 2
CH: Your approach, your weather
S: You are on an ILS approach and the RVR drops below minima. What do you do?
A: Depends where you are. Not commenced: don't commence if RVR/vis is below minimum. After commencing: don't continue below 1000 ft AAL or into the final approach segment if it falls below minimum.
S: But what if you are already past that point?
A: If already in the final approach segment or below 1000 ft AAL, you may continue to DA/H or MDA/H.
S: At DA - what decides if you land?
A: Required visual reference established at DA/H (or MDA/H) and maintained. Otherwise go around.
S: Which RVR reading do you trust if the three differ?
A: Touchdown zone RVR is always controlling; mid-point and stop-end are also controlling if reported and relevant.
CH: Cat I to Cat III
S: Define Cat I, II and III.
A: Cat I: DH not lower than 200 ft, vis >= 800 m or RVR >= 550 m. Cat II: DH < 200 ft but >= 100 ft, RVR >= 300 m. IIIA: DH < 100 ft or none, RVR >= 175 m. IIIB: DH < 50 ft or none, RVR 50-175 m. IIIC: no DH, no RVR limit.
S: What must be in place for Cat II/III?
A: Aircraft certified and equipped; DGCA approval; system recording auto-land success/failure; two pilots minimum; DH from radio altimeter; approved aerodrome.
S: What is alert height?
A: Height above runway threshold above which a Cat III approach is discontinued with a go-around if a redundant part of the landing system or ground equipment fails.
S: And what makes an autoland system fail-operational?
A: Approach, flare and landing can still be completed by the remaining automatic system after a failure (three autopilots).
CH: Low visibility take-off
S: What is a low visibility take-off?
A: A take-off with RVR less than 400 m.
S: What are the limits for scheduled operators?
A: Down to 125 m with DGCA approval, needing a 90 m visual segment from the cockpit at the start of the take-off run.
S: And non-scheduled/GA?
A: Not below 500 m RVR.
S: Can you convert met visibility to RVR for take-off?
A: No - conversion is not allowed for take-off, for any RVR minimum below 800 m, for visual/circling approaches, or when reported RVR is available.
CH: Restricted minima
S: What is Restricted AOM?
A: Normal DA/MDA +100 ft and visibility/RVR +400 m.
S: Who has to use it?
A: Non-scheduled/GA operators not authorised normal AOM, and PICs until 100 hours command experience on type.
S: What does a non-scheduled operator need for normal AOM?
A: PIC and co-pilot each 200 hours on type, 10 hours on type in last 90 days, and training including simulator min Level C.
@ 3
CH: Non-precision approaches done properly
S: How must NPAs be flown in India?
A: Using CDFA unless DGCA approves otherwise for that approach/runway.
S: What is CDFA?
A: Continuous descent from at or above the FAF to about 50 ft over the threshold, no level-off, consistent with stabilised approach.
S: What's the target descent angle and the max?
A: Optimum VDA 3.0 deg; max 4.5 deg Cat A/B, 3.77 deg Cat C/D.
S: What happens at MDA?
A: Only two options: continue below MDA with visual reference, or go around. A derived decision altitude (MDA + increment, min 50 ft) is used to start the missed approach so you don't dip below MDA.
S: Why do operators like it?
A: Safer via stabilised approach, less workload, better SA, fuel saving, less noise, reduced CFIT.
S: What if the operator does not use CDFA?
A: RVR penalty: +200 m Cat A/B, +400 m Cat C/D, capped at 5000 m.
@ 4
CH: Airspace classes
S: What is Class D?
A: IFR and VFR allowed, all get ATC. IFR separated from IFR and get traffic info on VFR; VFR get traffic info on all.
S: How is Class E different?
A: ATC only to IFR (separated from IFR); traffic info to all as far as practical. Not used for control zones.
S: Which classes does India use?
A: D, E, F, G. Delhi and Mumbai are Class D.
S: Speed limit?
A: 250 kt IAS for VFR in C and all traffic in D-G, below 10 000 ft.
S: Can VFR fly without a radio?
A: In classes E, F, G.
@ 5
CH: Radio failure in IMC
S: You lose comms in IMC. What first?
A: Try all other means to contact ATC, squawk 7600, and keep voice comms where CPDLC exists.
S: What level and speed do you fly?
A: Last assigned speed and level (or minimum flight altitude if higher).
S: For how long?
A: Non-radar: 20 minutes after missing a compulsory report. Radar: 7 minutes after the latest of reaching last level, selecting 7600, or missing a compulsory report. Then follow filed flight plan.
S: When do you descend and land?
A: Start descent at the fix at/near the last acknowledged EAT (else at ETA from flight plan); land if possible within 30 minutes of ETA.
@ 6
CH: EDTO explained
S: What is EDTO?
A: Operation by an aeroplane with two or more turbine engines where diversion time to an en-route alternate exceeds the DGCA threshold time.
S: What are the thresholds?
A: 60 min - twins over 45 360 kg and all scheduled twins; 90 min - non-scheduled under 45 360 kg; 120 min - more than two engines.
S: What fuel do you need?
A: EDTO critical fuel - the greatest of: decompression; decompression + engine failure; engine failure alone, at the most critical point.
S: What experience for 120 and 180 min approval?
A: 120: 12 consecutive months in-service with the airframe-engine combo. 180: 12 months holding 120 approval with high propulsion reliability.
S: What airfield can be an en-route alternate?
A: Adequate (facilities available) and suitable on the day (weather, serviceability). Take-off and destination may be considered en-route alternates.
@ 7
CH: Data link in oceanic
S: Why FANS?
A: Over oceans/poles there's no radar; procedural control over HF needs large separation. FANS (GNSS + satcom data link) gives a virtual radar environment.
S: Boeing vs Airbus?
A: FANS-1 Boeing; FANS-A Airbus.
S: What is CPDLC?
A: Controller-pilot data link: fixed-format messages through FMS/ATSU for all phases, including PDC, taxi, emergency messages.
S: ACARS vs VDL2?
A: ACARS 2.4 kbps; VDL Mode 2 31.5 kbps (10x), uses Mode S address, part of ATN.
@ 9
CH: TCAS from basics
S: What is TCAS?
A: Collision avoidance system independent of ATC.
S: TCAS I vs II?
A: I: TAs only. II: TAs and vertical RAs.
S: How does it work?
A: Secondary radar principle air-to-air on 1030/1090 MHz; two 3D bubbles; needs intruder transponder (min Mode A).
S: What are the timings?
A: TA 45-35 s (amber circle); RA 30-20 s (red square).
S: What do you do?
A: TA: visual search. RA: manoeuvre immediately, inform ATC when able of deviation.
S: Windshear, GPWS and TCAS at the same time?
A: Windshear first, GPWS second, TCAS third (Stall before all - SWAGAT).
@ 10
CH: GPWS to EGPWS
S: What is GPWS?
A: Computer using config, height and ILS data to warn of terrain with six modes.
S: Name the modes.
A: 1 excessive descent rate; 2 excessive terrain closure; 3 height loss after take-off; 4 flaps/gear not set; 5 below glide slope; 6 below minima.
S: How is EGPWS better?
A: Adds GPS and terrain database for look-ahead; 5A/5B, 6A/6B, mode 7 windshear.
S: Any weakness?
A: Only as good as its database - must be current.
@ 11
CH: Compass errors
S: What are the requirements of a DRMC?
A: Horizontal, sensitive, aperiodic.
S: Where is turning error greatest?
A: Turning through north and south; zero through east and west.
S: And acceleration error?
A: Zero on N/S; max on 090/270. Acceleration -> apparent turn toward nearer pole; deceleration -> toward the further pole.
CH: Gyroscope
S: What is a gyroscope?
A: A freely rotating rotor with rigidity and precession.
S: Define both.
A: Rigidity: axis stays fixed in space. Precession: moves at 90 degrees to applied force.
S: What affects them?
A: Rigidity proportional to I x omega; precession proportional to torque, inversely to I x omega.
S: Drift vs topple?
A: Both are wander: horizontal = drift; vertical = topple.
@ 12
CH: Inertial navigation
S: What is the principle of INS?
A: Self-contained; senses acceleration, integrates twice to velocity then distance.
S: What's different in IRS?
A: Ring laser gyros, strapdown, 0.5-2 nm/hr, 3-10 min alignment vs ~20 min for INS.
S: What's the laser gyro's disadvantage?
A: Laser lock at low rotation rates; dither motor vibrates to break it.
S: What does IRS need as inputs?
A: Initial position, barometric altitude and TAS.
@ 13
CH: RNAV and offsets
S: What is RNAV?
A: Navigation to required accuracy without flying over ground aids.
S: B-RNAV vs P-RNAV?
A: 5 nm vs 1 nm, 95% of the time.
S: How does the FMC fix position?
A: IRS + radio (twin DME) combined by Kalman filtering.
S: What is SLOP and why?
A: Offset to the right of centreline so opposite traffic doesn't line up - needed because high accuracy raises collision risk from altitude errors.
@ 16
CH: Polar flying
S: Why are polar regions hard?
A: Rapid convergency and magnetic variation changes, steep dip, unreliable compass, HF only, few alternates.
S: How do you navigate?
A: Grid navigation with gyro heading, INS/IRS, GPS; magnetic element of compass disconnected.
S: If two INS disagree?
A: Use malfunction codes and fix from radar/ADF/VOR; then ask nearby aircraft for spot winds/GS; last resort flight plan winds at DR.
@ 17
CH: North Atlantic MNPS
S: What is MNPS?
A: Minimum Navigation Performance Specification airspace over the North Atlantic, 35N-70N.
S: Which levels?
A: FL285-420 with RVSM FL290-410; Class A FL55-FL660 OCAs.
S: What equipment?
A: Two LRNS (GNSS/INS/IRS), RVSM equipment, ACAS II above 5700 kg turbine.
S: When do you get an oceanic clearance?
A: At least 40 min before entry from initial OACC.
S: Radio failure?
A: Maintain last acknowledged oceanic clearance to the last specified route point, then per state procedures (rejoin filed route if cleared otherwise).
S: Deviating around a storm without a clearance?
A: Move off OTS, broadcast on frequency and 121.5, watch TCAS, lights on; <10 NM keep level; >10 NM change +/-300 ft at about 10 NM.
S: If you cannot get any revised clearance?
A: Turn at least 45 deg, get 15 NM offset; above FL410 change 1000 ft, below FL410 500 ft; at FL410 climb 1000 or descend 500.
@ 18
CH: RNP vs RNAV
S: What is RNP?
A: Navigation accuracy statement with 95% containment; RNAV with on-board monitoring and alerting.
S: Examples?
A: RNP4 = within 4 NM 95% of the time. RNP values: 1, 4, 10, 12.6, 20.
S: NAT track spacing?
A: NAT is RNP20: 20+20+20 = 60 NM.
S: RNAV values in the approach?
A: IAF RNAV5; IAF-FAF RNAV1; FAF-landing RNAV0.3; missed approach RNAV1.
S: What do RNP10 and RNP4 need?
A: Two LRNS; R in Item 10; RNP4 uses GNSS and needs CPDLC+ADS-C for 30 NM separation.
@ 19
CH: RVSM
S: What is RVSM?
A: 1000 ft vertical separation between FL290 and FL410 inclusive; India since 27 Nov 2003.
S: What equipment?
A: Two independent altitude systems, Mode C, ACAS II, altitude alerting, automatic altitude control.
S: What tolerances?
A: Mean TVE 25 m (80 ft); level changes not overshoot/undershoot by more than 150 ft.
S: What if you lose capability?
A: Notify ATC (equipment failure, turbulence greater than moderate) - say "Unable RVSM due equipment/turbulence" and follow contingency.
@ 21
CH: Navigation basics
S: What's a great circle vs a rhumb line?
A: GC shortest path; rhumb line cuts meridians at a constant angle and lies nearer the Equator than the GC.
S: Why is GC track not constant?
A: Convergency.
S: What is the 1 in 60 rule?
A: 1 degree = 1 NM at 60 NM.
S: How do you use it for a glideslope?
A: 3 degrees = 300 ft/NM; ROD = 5 x groundspeed for 3 degrees.
@ 22
CH: Charts
S: Conditions for orthomorphism?
A: Meridians and parallels at right angles; scale same in all directions.
S: Describe Mercator.
A: Cylinder at equator; straight line is a rhumb line; used to 75 deg lat.
S: Why use Lambert?
A: GCs near straight, radio bearings plot without conversion angle, near-constant scale.
S: What about polar stereographic?
A: Light at opposite pole; only true perspective projection; used for polar areas.
@ 23
CH: Critical point and PNR
S: What's the Critical Point?
A: Equidistant in time between two airfields.
S: How does wind change it?
A: Moves into wind; still-air midpoint; more TAS acts like less wind.
S: PNR?
A: Furthest point you can fly and return to departure within safe endurance.
S: Effect of wind on PNR?
A: Larger wind component reduces distance to PNR; TAS up increases it.
@ 25
CH: Fuel
S: Fuel for a turbojet needing an alternate?
A: Destination + alternate + 30 min holding at 1500 ft AGL + 5% contingency.
S: Isolated aerodrome?
A: Destination + 2 hours at normal consumption.
S: Components of fuel?
A: Start & taxi, trip, contingency, alternate, final reserve (30 min jet / 45 piston), additional.
S: Hold specifics?
A: Right-hand turns standard; 1 min inbound leg at or below 14 000 ft, 1.5 min above.
@ 27
CH: Mass and balance
S: How is CG calculated?
A: Total moment / total mass.
S: Define DOM.
A: Aeroplane ready for operation excluding all fuel and traffic load: crew, catering, water, chemicals.
S: Ramp mass?
A: DOM + usable fuel + traffic load; take-off mass = ramp - start/taxi fuel.
S: Max traffic load?
A: MSTOM - DOM - take-off fuel.
@ 28
CH: Runway distances
S: Define TORA, TODA, ASDA.
A: TORA: take-off run; TODA = TORA + clearway (<=1.5 x TORA); ASDA = TORA + stopway.
S: What's a balanced field?
A: TODA = ASDA.
S: Clearway vs stopway?
A: Clearway: >=152 m wide, slope <=1.25%, obstacle-free for climb. Stopway: strength to support an abort.
S: ACN vs PCN?
A: ACN <= PCN to operate; 10% excess generally OK on good pavement.
@ 29
CH: V1 story
S: What is V1?
A: Decision speed - max speed to start stopping within ASDA (VSTOP), min speed to continue after engine failure within TODA (VGO).
S: What are its limits?
A: Not less than VEF + reaction gain, not less than VMCG, not more than VR, not more than VMBE.
S: What affects V1?
A: Mass up -> up; more flap -> down; density down -> up; tailwind/downslope -> down.
S: Why VMCG uses rudder only?
A: Ailerons would bank the wing into the ground; no nosewheel steering; density up -> VMCG up.
S: V2?
A: Take-off safety speed with one engine out by 35 ft; fly V2 until 400 ft.
@ 30
CH: Take-off flight path
S: Explain the four segments.
A: 1: 35 ft to gear up at V2. 2: gear up to 400 ft at V2 (2.4% min). 3: acceleration and flap retraction. 4: max continuous thrust to 1500 ft (1.2%).
S: Turn limits?
A: Not below half wingspan or 50 ft; 15 deg up to 400 ft; 25 deg above.
S: Obstacle margin?
A: 35 ft; 50 ft if turn over 15 deg.
@ 31
CH: ILS in detail
S: What are the components?
A: Localiser (VHF 108-111.975, odd first decimal), glidepath (UHF 329.15-335), markers (75 MHz).
S: How does the localiser work?
A: Two overlapping lobes, different modulation, DDM zero on centreline.
S: What are false glideslopes?
A: Additional zero-DDM paths from reflections; first at about 6 deg for a 3 deg GP; above true path, harmless.
S: Sensitivity?
A: LLZ 2.5 deg full scale (0.5 per dot); GP 0.7 deg full scale (0.14 per dot).
@ 33
CH: Radar and SSR
S: Primary vs secondary?
A: Primary: echo; secondary: transponder reply on another frequency.
S: SSR frequencies and modes?
A: 1030 up, 1090 down; Mode A identity, C altitude, S selective.
S: Special codes?
A: 7700 emergency, 7600 radio failure, 7500 hijack, 2000 no instructions.
S: What's garbling and fruiting?
A: Garbling: overlapping replies within ~1.7 nm; fruiting: replies to another radar's interrogation.
CH: Weather radar
S: AWR frequency and colours?
A: About 9375 MHz; black <0.7, green 0.7-4, yellow 4-12, red >12 mm/hr, magenta turbulence.
S: Cloud height?
A: Tilt until returns vanish, then 1 in 60.
S: Mapping?
A: Cosecant beam to ~70 nm, pencil beyond.
@ 34
CH: GNSS integrity
S: GPS segments?
A: Space, control, user; 24 SVs, 6 planes, ~20 180 km.
S: What is RAIM?
A: Receiver Autonomous Integrity Monitoring; needs five satellites.
S: Biggest error?
A: Ionospheric.
S: Augmentation types?
A: ABAS (onboard), GBAS (local), SBAS (wide area: WAAS, EGNOS, GAGAN).
@ 35
CH: Autoland
S: What does autoland need?
A: Two autopilots, two radio altimeters, Cat III ILS.
S: Fail-passive vs fail-operational?
A: Passive: safe but can't complete auto landing (two). Operational: still completes (three).
S: Radio altimeter?
A: FMCW, 2500 ft to 0; errors: fixed (5 ft steps) and mushing.
